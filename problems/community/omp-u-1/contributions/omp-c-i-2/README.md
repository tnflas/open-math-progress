# Why Is Ryser’s Conjecture Difficult? An Obstruction Atlas for Latin Transversals: Signed Weights, Local-Move Barriers, Structural Reductions, and Failed Shortcuts

**Problem ID:** `OMP-U-1`

**Authors:** Shuyang Xu

**Reported status:** Research report

**AI involvement:** AI-assisted

**OMP verification:** Not assessed

## Abstract

Ryser’s conjecture asserts that every Latin square of odd order admits a transversal.
This document is an exploratory, non-exhaustive map of obstacles to proving that asser-
tion, assembled from an AI-intensive collection of calculations, proposed arguments, finite
certificates, and negative results. Its intended contribution is pedagogical and diagnostic:
to give mathematicians concrete objects on which otherwise plausible methods fail, and to
make the outstanding logical interfaces explicit. It does not assert a proof or disproof of
Ryser’s conjecture, claim priority or novelty for any result, or certify all included arguments.
Most of the underlying investigation and much of the original wording were generated or
developed with AI assistance; mathematical claims should be checked independently before
use. The author does not intend to submit this survey to a refereed journal, and plans only
a public preprint.
The central worked example is a precise negative answer to a restricted local-positivity
question: on a fixed order-seven Latin support, even a family of integer kernel moves that
generates the entire kernel lattice need not connect every signed 1-weight of negative mass
one to a transversal while keeping all intermediate negative masses at most one. An exact
finite certificate exhibits a closed four-state component for all moves of ℓ1-norm at most
eight, although the square has 21 transversals and a four-step path exists once negative
mass two is allowed. The independent replay of the order-seven norm-six separator and two
inducedfive-core semigroups isalsosuppliedwithexact-checkscripts; theterm“independent”
here means a separately implemented computational check, not evaluation by an external
mathematical referee. A proposed infinite-product extension, lower-norm annihilating
functionals, partial-core semigroup holes, near-transversal exchange components, orthogonal-
mate constructions, and conditional EMM permutation-group reductions illustrate different
facets of the integral-versus-positive gap. Particular care is taken to distinguish this fixed-
support problem from the improper Latin square walk of Jacobson–Matthews and the
general Markov-basis connectivity theorem of Diaconis–Sturmfels. The author also outlines
a planned public GitHub research repository intended to facilitate the sharing, correction,
and independent verification of work related to Ryser’s conjecture.
